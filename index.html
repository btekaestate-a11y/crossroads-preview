The idea

Named for the place everyone passes through

Addis Ababa has Megenagna — the junction the whole city eventually moves through. Every city needs one: the spot that settles the where-are-we-meeting debate before it starts. Crossroads is built to be that answer for Washington.

Not a shrine and not a food court — a working room where the platter is shared, the coffee is a ceremony, and the evening itself is the point.

The lounge

Dinner is act one

Most restaurants end when the plates do. Crossroads is built like the junction it is named for — people arrive for dinner, collide with people they know, and stay for the part of the night where nobody checks the time.

Buna on the coals, tej in the glasses, a room that gets better as it gets louder.

፩

Sit down hungry

Come as a group or become part of one. Menus here are a negotiation — order for the table, not for yourself.

፪

Hands in

One platter lands in the middle. Tear the injera, scoop what is closest, trade corners with your neighbor.

፫

The jebena arrives

Coffee roasted in the room, brewed in the clay pot, poured in three rounds — abol, tona, baraka. Leaving after round one is technically allowed.

፬

The room turns over

Plates clear, lights drop, the volume finds its evening setting. This is the lounge half of the name.

House vocabulary

Words worth knowing before the first round

ቡና

buna — coffee, born in Ethiopia, roasted in the room and poured three times.

እንጀራ

injera — the sour flatbread that is plate, fork, and napkin in one.

ጉርሻ

gursha — feeding a friend by hand. The bigger the bite, the deeper the friendship.

ጠጅ

tej — honey wine. Golden, smooth, and not to be underestimated.

መገናኛ

megenagna — Addis Ababa’s famous junction; shorthand for where everyone meets.

ጥብስ

tibs — meat seared in a screaming-hot pan, best eaten immediately.

እስክስታ

eskista — the shoulder dance. It tends to find you after the second tej.

Learn two and use them at the table — the fastest known route to regular status.
